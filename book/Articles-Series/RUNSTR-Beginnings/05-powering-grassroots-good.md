## Writing Prompts for Dakota

### Introduction - The Charity Problem
1. What's broken about how most people give to charity?
2. Why do small, consistent donations matter more than occasional large ones?
3. What's the gap between wanting to help and actually helping that most people experience?
4. How did you think about charity before building RUNSTR vs. now?

### Body Paragraph 1 - The Organizations
5. What kinds of charities/organizations are in RUNSTR?
6. How do you choose which organizations to include?
7. What makes a "grassroots" organization different from a large charity?
8. Can you tell the story of one organization in RUNSTR - who they are, what they do?
9. How do you verify that organizations are legitimate and effective?

### Body Paragraph 2 - The Gamification
10. How does RUNSTR make giving feel like a game (in a good way)?
11. What's the Impact Level XP system and how does it work?
12. How do users see their cumulative impact over time?
13. What makes people want to keep contributing vs. lose interest?
14. How do teams/leaderboards drive collective giving?

### Body Paragraph 3 - The Math That Matters
15. If 1,000 RUNSTR users worked out daily for a year, how much would go to charity?
16. What could that amount actually accomplish for a small organization?
17. How does aggregated micro-giving compare to traditional charity approaches?
18. What's a real example of impact that small donations have created?

### Conclusion - The Movement
19. What's the long-term vision for RUNSTR's charitable impact?
20. How could this model change how people think about everyday actions?
21. What would you say to someone who thinks their small contribution doesn't matter?

## Key Themes to Weave In
- The power of aggregated small actions
- Grassroots organizations vs. charity overhead machines
- Making giving automatic, not a decision
- Building giving into lifestyle, not asking for sacrifice

- Real numbers: what X users working out Y times generates
