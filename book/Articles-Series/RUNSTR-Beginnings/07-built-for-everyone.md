## Writing Prompts for Dakota

### Introduction - The Real User
1. Describe the ideal RUNSTR user who has never heard of Bitcoin or Nostr.
2. What does this person care about? What are their fitness goals?
3. What frustrations do they have with their current fitness app?
4. Why would they switch to something new?

### Body Paragraph 1 - Simple by Design
5. How long does it take to get started with RUNSTR?
6. What does the onboarding experience look like for a complete newcomer?
7. What did you deliberately leave out to keep things simple?
8. How do you handle the complexity under the hood without exposing it?
9. What feedback have you gotten about ease of use?

### Body Paragraph 2 - What They Get
10. Walk through a typical week for an everyday user - what's the experience?
11. What features do normal users actually use vs. power users?
12. How does the reward experience work without understanding the underlying technology?
13. What makes someone keep coming back day after day?
14. What's the "aha moment" for most new users?

### Body Paragraph 3 - Why It Matters to Them
15. Why should someone who doesn't care about privacy actually benefit from it?
16. How does the charitable giving feature resonate with everyday people?
17. What emotional benefit does RUNSTR provide that other apps don't?
18. How does RUNSTR fit into someone's existing fitness routine?

### Conclusion - The Invitation
19. What would you say directly to someone reading this who's considering trying RUNSTR?
20. What's the lowest-friction way for someone to get started?
21. What do you hope their experience will be like in the first week?

## Key Themes to Weave In
- Accessibility without dumbing down
- The technology serves the user, not the other way around
- Meaningful fitness for everyday people
- Invitation, not gatekeeping

## Concrete Examples to Include
- Step-by-step first-time user experience
- Real or composite user stories
- Comparison to familiar apps (without bashing competitors)
- Specific features that "just work"

## User Personas to Consider
- **Sarah, 34**: Runs 3x/week, uses Strava, wants her fitness to mean something
- **Mike, 28**: Casual gym-goer, skeptical of apps, cares about privacy
- **Elena, 45**: Walks daily, wants to support causes, not tech-savvy
- **James, 22**: College student, tight budget, motivated by rewards

## Avoid
- Making it sound like users need to understand anything technical
- Condescending to non-technical users
- Over-explaining features
